How to Measure Your Ring Size at Home

The easiest way to measure your ring size at home is to measure the inside diameter of a ring that already fits the same finger, then match it to a US size chart (for example, 17.3 mm is a US size 7). If you have no ring to measure, wrap a thin strip of paper or string around the base of your finger, mark where it overlaps and measure the length in millimeters.

Why does ring size matter for a made to order ring?

A ring that is too tight is uncomfortable and hard to remove, and one that is too loose can slip off. Rings that are made to order in your size cannot be resized afterward, so the size you give before ordering is the size you will receive. Taking a few minutes to measure carefully is the best way to be happy with the fit.

How do you measure with a ring you already own?

Pick a ring that fits the finger you want to wear the new ring on, and that sits on the same part of the finger. Place it on a flat ruler and measure the inside diameter, from one inner edge to the opposite inner edge, in millimeters. Do not measure the outside of the band. Then find the closest number in the chart below. If your measurement falls between two sizes, choose the larger one for a wide band and tell us both numbers.

How do you measure with a strip of paper or string?

Cut a strip of paper about 6 mm wide and 15 cm long, or use a piece of thin string. Wrap it snugly around the base of the finger, with the end overlapping. Mark the exact point where the paper or string overlaps with a pen. Unwrap it, lay it flat and measure the length from the end to your mark in millimeters. That number is the circumference of your finger. Match it to the circumference column in the chart. Repeat two or three times and use the average, since it is easy to pull the strip too tight.

What is the US ring size chart in millimeters?

These are standard US sizes, with the inside diameter and the inside circumference in millimeters, rounded to one decimal place:

  • US 5: diameter 15.7 mm, circumference 49.3 mm
  • US 6: diameter 16.5 mm, circumference 51.9 mm
  • US 7: diameter 17.3 mm, circumference 54.4 mm
  • US 8: diameter 18.1 mm, circumference 57.0 mm
  • US 9: diameter 18.9 mm, circumference 59.5 mm
  • US 10: diameter 19.8 mm, circumference 62.1 mm

Charts from different brands can differ by a fraction of a millimeter, so treat these numbers as a guide and send us your measurement as well as your size.

When and how should you measure for the most accurate size?

Fingers change size during the day. They are usually smallest in the morning and in cold weather, and larger when it is hot, after exercise or after a salty meal. Measure at the end of the day when your hand is at normal temperature, and measure more than once on different days. A wide band feels tighter than a thin band, so if you are choosing a wide band, consider going up by a quarter or half size. Your knuckle matters too: the ring must pass over the knuckle but still stay in place at the base of the finger.

What if you are buying a ring as a gift or a proposal?

If you cannot ask, borrow a ring the person already wears on the same finger and measure its inside diameter, or trace the inside of the ring on paper. You can also ask a close friend or family member for help. Because rings are made to order here, please double check the size before you place the order.
